# Assessment of the next measurement stage

The six proposals motivate useful experiments. They are not validated assumptions about this apparatus. Core thickness processing remains unchanged; signal-pattern features are opt-in. The equipment was not connected during this work.

## 1. Thin-film filtering, baseline fit and temperature

Run `python examples/audit_thin_layer.py`. It writes synthetic results to `results/thin-layer-audit.json`. It compares raw, causal first-order highpass, causal bandpass, zero-phase bandpass, and bandpass plus a 5.96 MHz/Q=30 notch. It sweeps three pulse widths, two carrier frequencies, and 0.3/0.5 mm delays. The controlled least-squares experiment uses a known acrylic position to isolate amplitude bias. It does not implement or validate a replacement alignment/detection pipeline. A second experiment runs the unchanged baseline detector on independent synthetic test frames and small shape stretches.

**Pre-ringing is real, but a causal filter alone is not a demonstrated solution.** A compact-support synthetic pulse has zero pre-onset RF energy with raw/causal filtering and nonzero energy with forward/reverse filtering. Real acoustic pulses also have a leading tail before their envelope maximum, which a causal filter cannot remove. In the 3.2 MHz, 0.25 microsecond Gaussian-width, 0.3 mm example, the known-position fit has about -1.79% scale bias with the existing bandpass, -1.54% raw, and -1.04% with the causal bandpass. These are example-specific, not an accuracy ranking of complete detectors. Causal filtering shifts and reshapes the pulse; the template, alignment anchor, search windows and held-out residual thresholds would all need consistent recalibration.

`butter(4, ..., btype='bandpass')` produces an eighth-order bandpass (four SOS sections); forward/reverse filtering squares its magnitude response. Calling it only a fourth-order zero-phase filter obscures that distinction. See [SciPy butter](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.signal.butter.html) and [sosfiltfilt](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.signal.sosfiltfilt.html).

**Keep the notch experimental.** Q=30 at 5.96 MHz means a nominal one-pass bandwidth of approximately 199 kHz, using [SciPy's definition](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.signal.iirnotch.html). It also introduces a resonant time scale of roughly Q/(pi*f), about 1.6 microseconds. A lower noise RMS does not prove unchanged thin-echo timing, tails or false-detection rate. The audit records noise RMS, pulse FWHM, tail energy, fit leakage and scale bias. Its interference is deliberately injected; it does not prove that the real apparatus currently contains this tone. Record a fresh noise spectrum, inspect drift of the tone, and rebuild baseline thresholds with exactly the candidate chain before an A/B comparison. No notch is enabled in live processing.

**Do not convert a guessed acrylic coefficient into an operational temperature limit.** For an acrylic path with delay t, thermal expansion alpha and velocity derivative dc/dT, first-order propagation gives dt/dT = t*(alpha - (dc/dT)/c). As an illustrative assumption only, t=3.744 microseconds, c=2670 m/s and dc/dT=-2.5 m/s/degree C give about 3.5 ns/degree C before expansion. An uncorrected 4 ns delay budget would then suggest about 1.1 degrees C, but that is not a validated tolerance after shift fitting or for pulse shape/coupling. Water speed, gradients, coupling and attenuation also change. Measure the coefficient for the actual acrylic and temperature range; use the residual mismatch diagnostic and references before/after a session. No universal DeltaT maximum is claimed.

The synthetic +/-0.2% shape-stretch experiment alone does not justify adding a fitted stretch parameter. Extra fit freedom can absorb the desired overlapping echo and falsely reduce the residual. Keep fresh baselines and shift/scale fitting for now. Evaluate a constrained stretch on independent thick-water controls, genuine thin layers and changed-coupling negatives before enabling it. The example's stretch is not a calibrated temperature perturbation.

## 2. Internal echoes

With `--flow-features`, `EchoDSP.detect()` searches for resolved secondary peaks after plate-reverberation rejection and before the unchanged strongest/main echo. The initial height floor is max(adaptive/manual threshold, 15% of main amplitude), prominence is half that floor, and minimum separation is 0.8 microseconds. These are experimental settings in `EchoConfig`, not physical constants. Candidates within 0.15 microseconds (`secondary_plate_edge_us`) of samples zeroed by plate rejection are dropped: the hard zeroing leaves the reverberation's envelope just outside the rejected window, which otherwise appeared as an internal peak when the surface echo was weaker than the reverberation.

**Plate rejection is opt-in.** Without `--pantulan-us` / `--preset akrilik5mm`, plate reverberations are not removed and can be counted as internal peaks when the surface echo is weak (below roughly 40% of the acrylic echo); the CLI prints a warning and the metadata records `plate_rejection`. On the existing 20/40 mm recordings (surface echo 0.8-1.5x the acrylic echo) the false internal rate was 0.0-0.1% either way.

`tof_internal_us` reports the earliest qualifying candidate; `n_peaks` counts those candidates plus the main echo. Later echoes are excluded. An internal echo can be a bubble, reverberation, scattering or an artefact. The algorithm still cannot prove that its main echo is the surface if a bubble is stronger. It cannot resolve arbitrarily close peaks or infer bubble size/count from peak count. Baseline mode does not perform this analysis: it reports unavailable internal-echo statistics, not confirmed absence of bubbles.

## 3. Rolling pattern indicators

`FlowRegimeClassifier` uses positive valid thickness, amplitude and candidate presence on one of two time bases. **Sequence (default, `--flow-timebase sequence`):** frame time is the server sequence divided by a frame rate that is validated on every computation. A contiguous sequence only proves that no frame was lost; it is not evidence of uniform device sampling or of the nominal rate. The window is split into quarters; per quarter the lower envelope (least-delayed frame) of `receive time - sequence/--fps` is taken, and the overall, early (Q1-Q2) and late (Q3-Q4) rates from its slope must all lie within 5% of `--fps`. Otherwise the window is `TIMING_UNCERTAIN` without frequency, and it stays so until the whole window was received after the failure; no earlier frequency is carried on. The validated overall rate is used for the axis. Before a full window exists the label is `WARMUP`. **Receive (`--flow-timebase receive`):** PC receive times are used directly and irregular intervals are rejected. Receive times now come from `time.perf_counter()`. Recordings made before this change used `time.monotonic()`, which on Windows is GetTickCount64 with 15.625 ms resolution: in the lab recordings 78.6% of frame intervals were zero, every window was `TIMING_UNCERTAIN`, and equal timestamps cleared the window. Replay those recordings with the sequence time base.

Limits of the timing validation (synthetic tests only, no device timestamps): PC receive times include network and OS delay, not device acquisition time. With exponential transport delays of up to 20 ms mean (with or without the 15.625 ms clock) a true 300 frames/s stream was still classified (3 Hz wave reported as 2.98-3.03 Hz); at 50 ms mean most windows became `TIMING_UNCERTAIN`. Rate errors of 10% (270 or 330 frames/s) were always rejected; errors of about 3% lie inside the tolerance and are corrected by the estimated rate (2.985-3.013 Hz for a 3 Hz wave), but early/late quarter estimates fluctuate by about 6%, so labels can alternate with `TIMING_UNCERTAIN` near the tolerance. A rate change shorter than about a quarter window, drift inside the tolerance, or irregular device sampling hidden behind a steady transport delay is not detected; a hardware trigger timestamp would be needed. It updates at most 10 times/s over two seconds by default; `--flow-window 5` allows more evidence for 0.5 Hz waves. A two-second record has 0.5 Hz bin spacing but only one cycle at 0.5 Hz. Frequency estimation needs roughly two observed cycles; interpolating FFT bins does not create new resolution.

| Output | Initial rule and interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| `SMOOTH_CANDIDATE` | mean thickness >2 mm, sigma <0.05 mm, amplitude CV <0.1; may be static water |
| `THIN_STABLE_CANDIDATE` | mean thickness <2 mm with the same stability checks; does not establish annular geometry |
| `WAVY_CANDIDATE` | thickness >2 mm, sigma >0.2 mm, a concentrated detrended 0.5–15 Hz spectral peak, and sufficient cycles |
| `INTERMITTENT_CANDIDATE` | amplitude CV >0.35 together with internal candidates in >=5% of valid frames or pronounced negatively skewed thickness spikes; does not distinguish bubbly from slug |
| `INTERNAL_ECHO_CANDIDATE` | persistent internal peaks without the amplitude condition above |
| `UNKNOWN` / quality status | insufficient separation of hypotheses, warmup, invalid detections, sequence discontinuity or unreliable timing |

At least 90% of the window must contain valid observations. An invalid current frame is labelled `INVALID_DATA` for that row only; it no longer replaces the latest window result (which previously held `INVALID_DATA` for 0.1 s per invalid frame, so 5% scattered invalid frames left only about 21% of valid rows classified). Gaps of more than 0.1 s of invalid frames, a lost sequence number or a clock going backwards suppress classification; in the receive time base strongly irregular intervals do too. Network timestamps still limit physical frequency interpretation even when these checks pass. RF averaging can suppress short bubble/spike events; record `--rata`. Features use pre-display-median detections. In baseline mode, residual amplitude has a different meaning; CV thresholds need independent calibration there.

Two-phase regime identification needs labeled experiments, geometry/orientation, operating conditions and independent visualization/reference sensors. Annular flow describes phase distribution around a conduit; a single thin local film cannot establish that. Stable thickness does not prove flow. Report confusion matrices and false positives on held-out sessions rather than treating four passing synthetic tests as physical validation.

## 4. Wave speed versus flow rate

One normal-incidence pulse-echo sensor measures local thickness evolution. Mean thickness, dominant frequency and wave height do not uniquely determine velocity or volumetric flow. Two separated, correctly timed observations can estimate the **propagation speed of correlated structures**, v_wave = Delta x / Delta tau. That is generally not the cross-sectional mean liquid velocity. Discharge needs the occupied area and mean velocity, or a separately validated model/calibration.

The current native protocol returns one channel. `single-ch1` GPIO selection does not already provide synchronized dual-channel acquisition, and IN2 alone does not supply a second transmit/receive path. Design the second transducer path, common trigger, channel identity, hardware timestamps, switching/settling delay and crosstalk tests before modifying firmware/protocol. For correlation, detrend both records, limit physically plausible lags, require enough overlap, reject flat/periodic ambiguous peaks, and report lag uncertainty and decorrelation. Multiplexing two sensors at 300 total shots/s is not automatically 300 samples/s per sensor. No two-channel operation or flow-rate output is claimed here. Related experimental context: [ultrasonic monitoring of interfacial motion](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0955598605000683).

## 5. Physical proposals for the next lab session

**Surfactant:** better wetting is a hypothesis worth testing, but '<0.05%' does not specify chemical identity, mass/volume basis or relation to critical micelle concentration. Surfactants change interfacial dynamics and can change foaming, acoustic velocity and attenuation; 'no change in c' cannot be assumed. Research directly measures concentration/temperature-dependent acoustic properties in [nonionic surfactant solutions](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0301010416306577) and [foamability versus formulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.07869). Treat treated water as a different experimental fluid. Record formulation, concentration basis and temperature; recalibrate sound speed and baseline with that fluid; compare untreated controls. A wetting-aided static thickness calibration is not automatically representative of the original two-phase flow.

**Limiter/damping:** determine which stage clips first and whether the 2–3 microsecond disturbance is electrical overload, receiver recovery, mechanical ringing or multipath. A limiter cannot recover ADC samples already clipped or eliminate acoustic ringing by itself. Diode capacitance, voltage/current-dependent conduction, recovery, source impedance and resistor placement affect the wanted RF echo too; a 50–100 ohm series resistor and a shunt termination have very different effects. 1N4148 and BAT54 are not interchangeable clamps with a fixed universal threshold: consult the [1N4148 data sheet](https://assets.nexperia.com/documents/data-sheet/1N4148_1N4448.pdf) and [BAT54 data sheet](https://assets.nexperia.com/documents/data-sheet/BAT54.pdf).

The [STEMlab 125-14 specifications](https://redpitaya.com/stemlab-125-14/) list selectable +/-1 V and +/-20 V full-scale input ranges and 1 megaohm input impedance. Confirm the exact board/revision, jumper setting, receive amplifier, transducer/pulser and intended network before choosing protection. Full-scale range is not an invitation to exceed absolute maximum ratings. Bench-characterize the conditioner with a suitable probe/attenuator and known pulses before reconnecting the ADC: clamp current/voltage, overshoot, echo insertion loss, recovery and phase response. No component values or topology are approved by this software change. Keep raw control recordings before/after any hardware modification and rebuild baselines.
